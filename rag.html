Understanding RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

Standard Large Language Models (LLMs) rely on fixed training data. They cannot see your internal company docs, private notes, or real-time news without expensive fine-tuning.

The 5 Steps Simulated in This Tool:
  1. Document Chunking: Raw text is divided into smaller overlapping blocks so we can search specific passages.
  2. Embedding Generation: Each chunk is converted by a neural net into a list of numbers (vectors) representing its meaning.
  3. Vector Database Storage: Embeddings are stored in specialized databases (like Pinecone, Chroma, FAISS).
  4. Semantic Search: When you search, your question is converted into a vector. The database finds chunks with similar math vectors (Cosine Similarity).
  5. Prompt Augmentation: The top matching chunks are glued into the LLM prompt as reference material to build a grounded answer.
Traditional Search

Keyword matching (BM25). Fails if synonym is used (e.g., searches for "car" but doc says "automobile").

Semantic RAG Search

Vector distance matching. Understands intent, context, and concepts across vocabulary differences.
